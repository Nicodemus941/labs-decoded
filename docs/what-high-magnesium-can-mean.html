Magnesium

What high magnesium can mean

Magnesium is an electrolyte that helps your muscles, nerves, and heart work properly. A high blood magnesium result is called hypermagnesemia, but the number still needs context before anyone can say what it means for you.

Short answer: High magnesium is uncommon and is most often linked to trouble clearing magnesium through the kidneys or to magnesium- containing products such as supplements, laxatives, or antacids. Providers usually interpret the result alongside symptoms, kidney function, medicines, and other lab tests.
Updated August 16, 2026

What a magnesium test measures

MedlinePlus explains that a magnesium blood test measures the amount of magnesium in a blood sample. Magnesium is a mineral and an electrolyte, and your body uses it to support muscle function, nerve signaling, heart function, blood pressure, and blood sugar control.

Only a small amount of your body's magnesium is in the bloodstream at any one time. Most of it is stored in bones and other tissues, so a blood result is useful but still only one piece of the larger picture.

Why magnesium can run high

High magnesium is not one of the most common abnormal lab results. MedlinePlus says hypermagnesemia is uncommon, and Cleveland Clinic describes it as a rare electrolyte disorder.

  • Kidneys are not clearing extra magnesium well.
  • Magnesium-containing supplements, antacids, or laxatives add too much.
  • Some prescription medicines, including lithium, can play a role.
  • Some medical conditions such as dehydration, adrenal problems, or hypothyroidism can be part of the picture.

Both MedlinePlus and Cleveland Clinic point to kidney failure or major loss of kidney function as the most common reason levels rise. Cleveland Clinic also notes that people with kidney disease are at higher risk if they take products that contain magnesium.

Why supplements and over-the-counter products matter

NIH's Office of Dietary Supplements says magnesium from food is not usually harmful in healthy people because the kidneys can get rid of extra amounts in urine. The bigger concern is magnesium from supplements or medicines.

MedlinePlus specifically lists magnesium-containing laxatives and antacids as products that can raise blood magnesium. Cleveland Clinic makes the same point and adds that long-term use of magnesium-containing products can become a problem, especially when kidney clearance is already impaired.

What symptoms may happen when magnesium is high

Mild cases may cause no symptoms. Cleveland Clinic says symptoms become more noticeable as levels rise and may include dizziness, nausea, confusion, weakness, or trouble breathing.

Severe cases can affect the heart and nervous system. MedlinePlus and NIH both note that very high magnesium can be linked to dangerous heart rhythm problems, and Cleveland Clinic adds that severe toxicity can progress to cardiac arrest.

How providers usually interpret the result

A high magnesium result is rarely read in isolation. MedlinePlus explains that abnormal magnesium levels can help diagnose several disorders and may be checked alongside calcium, potassium, and phosphorus because magnesium helps affect how the body handles those minerals.

Clinicians usually want to know whether you have symptoms, how your kidneys are working, what medicines or supplements you take, and whether nearby labs also look abnormal.

  • Kidney function tests and hydration status.
  • Medication, laxative, antacid, and supplement history.
  • Related electrolyte results such as calcium and potassium.
  • Whether the result fits with symptoms or seems out of proportion.

How to read the reference range

MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia lists a typical serum magnesium reference range of 1.7 to 2.2 mg/dL, but it also says normal ranges can vary slightly between laboratories.

That means the range printed on your own report matters more than any single generic cutoff you find online. A result can look borderline in one lab and clearly high in another.

Questions you may want to ask at follow-up

  • How high was the result compared with this lab's reference range?
  • Could supplements, antacids, laxatives, or another medicine be affecting it?
  • Was kidney function also abnormal?
  • Do I need repeat testing or a review of related electrolytes?

FAQ

Does high magnesium always mean kidney failure?

No. Kidney problems are the most common reason high magnesium shows up, but magnesium-containing supplements, antacids, laxatives, and some other conditions can also contribute.

Can food alone usually cause high magnesium?

Usually not in otherwise healthy people. NIH says magnesium that is naturally present in food is not harmful because healthy kidneys can remove extra magnesium in urine.

Why might my provider ask about supplements or antacids after a high magnesium result?

Because several magnesium-containing products can raise magnesium levels, especially if the kidneys are not clearing magnesium well.
