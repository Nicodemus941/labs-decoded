Albumin

What low albumin can mean

Albumin is a protein made by your liver. It helps keep fluid inside your blood vessels and also helps carry hormones, vitamins, medicines, and other substances through the body.[1][2][4]

Short answer: A low albumin result can happen when the liver is not making enough albumin, when the kidneys are letting albumin leak out, or when another condition such as inflammation, infection, digestive disease, or poor nutrition is affecting protein levels. One low result is a clue, not a diagnosis by itself.[1][2][3][4]
Updated August 5, 2026

What albumin measures

MedlinePlus says an albumin blood test measures how much albumin is in your blood. Cleveland Clinic describes albumin as a protein in blood plasma that your liver makes and that helps stop fluid from leaking out of the bloodstream.[1][2]

That is why albumin often comes up in liver panels, comprehensive metabolic panels, and other workups related to swelling, liver disease, kidney disease, or nutrition concerns.[1][2][4]

What counts as low

Cleveland Clinic says a typical adult albumin range is about 3.5 to 5.5 grams per deciliter, but exact ranges can vary by lab. A flagged result should be read against the lab's own reference range and the rest of the testing pattern.[2]

Common reasons albumin runs low

Low albumin does not point to only one cause. MedlinePlus says lower-than-normal albumin levels may be a sign of liver disease, kidney disease, malnutrition, infection, digestive diseases involving poor protein absorption, severe burns, and thyroid disease.[1]

  • Liver disease or liver damage.[1][3]
  • Kidney disease or albumin loss into urine.[1][2][4]
  • Malnutrition or not getting enough protein.[1][2][4]
  • Digestive or malabsorption conditions.[1][4]
  • Infection, inflammation, surgery, or burns.[1][2]

Mayo Clinic also notes that low albumin and low total protein can show up in liver disease, but can also be seen in gastrointestinal and kidney-related conditions. That is why clinicians usually avoid reading albumin in isolation.[3]

Why swelling can happen with low albumin

One of albumin's main jobs is helping keep fluid in the bloodstream. MedlinePlus says that if you do not have enough albumin, fluid can leak out of your blood vessels and build up in your lungs, abdomen, or other parts of the body.[1]

That is one reason swelling in the legs, ankles, feet, face, or belly often matters in the same conversation as a low albumin result.[1][2][4]

What else providers often look at

Both MedlinePlus and Cleveland Clinic say an albumin result alone cannot diagnose a condition. Providers usually compare it with other liver and kidney tests, urine testing, symptoms, medicines, and sometimes total protein or the albumin/globulin ratio.[1][2][4]

  • Were other liver tests or total protein abnormal too?[1][3][4]
  • Is albumin showing up in the urine, which can point toward kidney loss?[1][2]
  • Are swelling, jaundice, dark urine, or appetite and weight changes part of the picture?[1][2][4]
  • Could nutrition, digestion, infection, inflammation, or burns be involved?[1][2][4]

FAQ

Does low albumin always mean liver disease?

No. Low albumin can happen with liver disease, but MedlinePlus and Cleveland Clinic also list kidney disease, malnutrition, infection, inflammation, digestive disease, and other conditions as possible explanations.[1][2][4]

Why can low albumin make you swell?

Albumin helps keep fluid from leaking out of blood vessels. When albumin is low, fluid may collect in tissues instead, which is why swelling or puffiness can show up.[1][2]

What tests may come up after a low albumin result?

Follow-up often includes a liver panel, kidney-related testing, urine testing, and sometimes total protein or the A/G ratio so the result can be interpreted in context.[1][2][3][4]
